# reasoning.plasma.surface_waves_plasmonics — Interface → SPP

## Core Picture

At a flat interface between a dielectric (ε_d > 0) and a plasma/metal
(ε_p(ω) < 0 for ω < ω_p), a surface plasmon polariton (SPP) exists:
a coupled EM wave + plasma oscillation that propagates ALONG the interface
and decays exponentially PERPENDICULAR to it.

## Derivation Sketch (from spatial dispersion → surface-bound modes)

Starting from `landau-graph: reasoning.spatial_dispersion` (which
establishes that ε(k,ω) depends on wavevector, enabling nonlocal response
at interfaces), the SPP is the specific interface mode:

1. **Matching condition at a planar interface**: Maxwell's equations with
   piecewise-constant ε demand continuity of E_∥, D_⊥, H_∥, B_⊥. For TM
   polarization (the only one supporting SPPs), these yield:
   k_z1/ε_d + k_z2/ε_p = 0, where k_zi = √(k² − ε_i ω²/c²) is the
   decay constant in medium i. KEY NON-OBVIOUS: this is the same equation
   as for any TM waveguide mode, but the sign of ε_p makes k_z positive
   real in BOTH media (decaying, not oscillating) — the mode is BOUND.

2. **Solving k_z1/ε_d + k_z2/ε_p = 0** → SPP dispersion:
   k_SPP(ω) = (ω/c) √[ε_d ε_p/(ε_d + ε_p)].
   The denominator ε_d + ε_p < 0 must be negative for k to be real,
   which requires ε_p < −ε_d. For a Drude metal ε_p=1−ω_p²/ω², this
   means ω < ω_p/√(1+ε_d). At ω → ω_p/√(1+ε_d), k_SPP → ∞ and the
   mode becomes highly localized (electrostatic surface plasmon).

3. **Time-domain plasmon dynamics** (attosecond/nano-optics): in the
   time domain, an SPP pulse of duration τ has bandwidth Δω ∼ 1/τ.
   When τ < 10 fs, the SPP dispersion curvature ∂²k/∂ω² causes pulse
   broadening and reshaping during propagation. The group velocity
   v_g = dω/dk varies strongly near the surface plasmon frequency —
   SPPs can be slowed to v_g ≪ c (slow light). For attosecond plasmonics,
   the field variation is faster than the electron relaxation time
   (τ_e-e ∼ 1-10 fs), so the plasmonic response becomes non-adiabatic;
   the Drude model breaks down — use the nonlocal hydrodynamic model
   (density functional theory for sub-nm features).

4. **Strong-field plasmonics**: when the local field enhancement
   |E_loc|/|E₀| ∼ 10-100 (LSPR or SPP focusing), the electric field
   at nanotips can reach >10 V/nm, exceeding the atomic field
   E_at = e/(4πε₀ a₀²) ≈ 5×10¹¹ V/m. This drives tunnel ionization,
   field emission, and optical-field electron emission. The plasma
   is then self-generated by the same field that excites the plasmon.
   Modeling requires coupled Maxwell-plasma (PIC) plus quantum tunneling
   (Fowler-Nordheim for DC, Keldysh for optical). The metallic ε(ω)
   becomes intensity-dependent (Kerr nonlinearity χ⁽³⁾ of the metal itself).

5. **Phonon/exciton-polaritons (strong coupling)**: the SPP dispersion
   anti-crosses with material resonances. For a phonon resonance at ω_TO:
   ε_p(ω) = ε_∞ + (ε_0−ε_∞) ω_TO²/(ω_TO²−ω²−iΓω) (Lorentz model).
   The SPP + phonon hybridize into surface phonon-polaritons with an
   anti-crossing (Rabi splitting Ω_R ∝ √(ε_0−ε_∞)). Same physics for
   exciton-polaritons (strong coupling regime: Ω_R > γ_SPP + γ_exciton).
   This enters the ultra-strong coupling (USC) regime when Ω_R/ω₀ > 0.1,
   where the rotating wave approximation fails and the ground state
   contains virtual photons (Bloch-Siegert shift, photon condensation).

## SPP Dispersion (Shah §2-3, Raether §2)

For a single flat interface (dielectric | Drude metal):

```
k_SPP = (ω/c) √[ε_d ε_p(ω) / (ε_d + ε_p(ω))]
ε_p(ω) = 1 − ω_p²/ω²    (Drude model, no damping)
```

**Existence condition**: ε_d + ε_p(ω) < 0 → ω < ω_p/√(1+ε_d).
For metal/vacuum: ω < ω_p/√2. For metal/dielectric: cutoff reduced.

**Confinement** (1/e decay lengths):
- Into dielectric: δ_d = (1/k) √[1 − ε_d/ε_p] (longer)
- Into plasma/metal: δ_p = (1/k) √[1 − ε_p/ε_d] (shorter, ∼10-50 nm)

**Dispersion character**: at low k, SPP ≈ light line (ω≈ck/√ε_d).
At high k, SPP → ω_p/√(1+ε_d) (asymptotic surface plasmon frequency).
Momentum mismatch with free-space light → needs grating or prism coupling.

## Excitation Methods

- **Kretschmann** (prism coupling): thin metal film on prism, ATR dip.
- **Otto**: prism separated by gap from metal surface, evanescent coupling.
- **Grating coupling**: periodic surface provides Δk = 2πn/Λ matching.
- **Near-field** (SNOM): sub-wavelength probe excitation.

## Localized Surface Plasmons (LSPR)

**Nanoparticles** (sub-wavelength): no propagating SPP, instead localized
oscillation. For spherical NP in uniform field:
α = 4πa³ (ε_p−ε_d)/(ε_p+2ε_d). Resonance at Re[ε_p] = −2ε_d.
Field enhancement: |E_loc|/|E₀| ∼ |3ε_d/(ε_p+2ε_d)|. LSPR frequency depends
on size, shape, material, environment. Used in: SERS (enhancement ∼10⁶-10⁸),
biosensing (shift with binding), color engineering (stained glass).

## Reserved Interfaces

- **Quantum plasmonics**: nonlocal response (k_∥ ∼ k_F → δ ∼ λ_F), electron
  spill-out, tunneling across sub-nm gaps, quantum-size effects in NP.
- **Strong-field plasmonics**: field emission at sharp tips, tunnel ionization,
  optical-field electron emission, attosecond nanoplasmonics.
- **Phonon/exciton-polaritons**: strong coupling regime (Rabi splitting
  Ω_R > γ_SPP + γ_exciton) at mid-IR (phonon) and visible (exciton).
  Ultra-strong coupling (Ω_R/ω₀ > 0.1) breaks the rotating wave approximation;
  ground state contains virtual excitations.

## Edge Cases

- **Interband transitions**: Noble metals (Ag, Au, Cu) have d→sp transitions
  at visible frequencies (Ag: ∼3.8 eV, Au: ∼2.4 eV). The Drude model fails —
  ε_p(ω) becomes complex with Re[ε_p] > −ε_d, suppressing SPP. Use measured
  optical constants (Johnson & Christy, Palik) instead. For Au at λ<500 nm,
  interband absorption quenches the SPP — use Ag or Al for blue/UV plasmonics.
- **Nonlocal response**: For gaps < 1 nm or features < λ_F (∼0.5 nm in Au),
  the local ε approximation fails — requires quantum treatment (time-dependent
  DFT, quantum hydrodynamic model). The nonlocal correction shifts the LSPR
  blue and introduces electron spill-out reducing the gap permittivity.
  When d_gap < 0.3 nm, electron tunneling shorts the plasmon — use
  quantum-corrected model (QCM) with Feibelman d-parameters.
- **Gain media**: ε_p'(ω) > 0 (pumped) → loss-compensated or amplifying SPP.
  The gain threshold g_th = (c/ω) k_SPP'' compensates Ohmic loss. Gain
  saturation limits the achievable amplification. When gain exceeds threshold,
  the SPP becomes a SPASER (surface plasmon amplification by stimulated
  emission of radiation) — a coherent nanoscale source. The Drude + gain
  model breaks down near the lasing threshold where the linewidth narrowing
  violates the rate-equation approximation — use semi-classical laser theory.
- **Ultra-strong coupling**: Rabi splitting > 10% ω₀ → SPP dispersion
  bifurcates into upper/lower polariton branches. The rotating-wave
  approximation fails; counter-rotating terms (a†b† and ab) become important.
  The ground state is no longer the vacuum — it contains virtual photons.
  Observable signatures: Bloch-Siegert shift, modified Lamb shift, and
  possibility of photon pair emission from the dressed ground state.
  Use the full quantum Rabi model (not Jaynes-Cummings) when Ω_R/ω₀ > 0.1.
- **Time-domain / attosecond regime**: when the SPP pulse duration τ < 10 fs,
  the quasi-monochromatic (dω/ω ≪ 1) approximation breaks down. The full
  time-domain Maxwell + Drude-Lorentz model with ∂P/∂t dynamics is needed.
  For τ < 1 fs (attosecond), the electron gas cannot respond adiabatically;
  the Drude model's instantaneous polarization assumption fails — use the
  nonlocal hydrodynamic model or time-dependent DFT. Surface plasmons
  excited by attosecond pulses probe the build-up of collective response
  on the ~100 as timescale of electron plasma oscillations.
